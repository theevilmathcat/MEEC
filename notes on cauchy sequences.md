# Notes on Cauchy Sequences

A running collection of my notes on Cauchy sequences, convergence, and completeness.

---

## Cauchy Sequences

- A **Cauchy sequence** means the terms are getting arbitrarily close to each other as you go down the list.
- It only depends on the **distance between the terms themselves**. A sequence can be Cauchy regardless of whether its final destination exists in your number set.

## Limits and Convergence

- The **limit** is the specific value that the terms are marching toward; in a complete space every Cauchy sequence has such a limit in the space, but in an incomplete space the limit may be missing.
- A sequence **converges** in a given space only if its limit actually lives inside that space. If the limit point is missing, the sequence is still Cauchy, but it fails to converge in that space.

## Completeness

- If some Cauchy sequence does not converge in a space, the space is said to be **non-complete**.
- If every Cauchy sequence does converge, the space is **complete**.
